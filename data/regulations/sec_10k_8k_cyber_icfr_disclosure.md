# SEC Cybersecurity Incident Disclosure (Form 8-K Item 1.05 & Form 10-K Item 1C) & SOX ICFR

## Form 8-K Item 1.05 — Material Cybersecurity Incidents (`17 CFR § 249.308`)

### Mandatory 4-Business-Day Reporting Deadline (`Form 8-K Item 1.05(a)`)
If a registrant experiences a **cybersecurity incident** that is determined by the registrant to be **material**, the registrant must describe the material aspects of the nature, scope, and timing of the incident, and the material impact or reasonably likely material impact on the registrant, including its financial condition and results of operations, by filing a **Form 8-K under Item 1.05**.

1. **Filing Timeline (`4 Business Days from Materiality Determination`)**:
   An Item 1.05 Form 8-K must be filed **within four (4) business days** after the registrant **determines that it has experienced a material cybersecurity incident**.
   - **Critical Timing Rule**: The 4-business-day clock starts upon the **determination of materiality**, NOT the date of discovery of the incident. However, Instruction 1 to Item 1.05 mandates that the registrant must make its materiality determination **"without unreasonable delay"** after discovery of the incident.
   - **Violation Warning**: Delaying a materiality determination while waiting for a forensic investigation to conclude, or postponing filing beyond 4 business days due to internal remediation or law enforcement notification (without a formal U.S. Attorney General written determination), is a direct violation of `Form 8-K Item 1.05`.

2. **Exclusive National Security / Public Safety Delay Exception (`Form 8-K Item 1.05(c)`)**:
   The filing of an Item 1.05 Form 8-K may be delayed **only if the United States Attorney General** determines in writing that immediate disclosure would pose a **substantial risk to national security or public safety** and notifies the Commission (SEC) of such determination in writing:
   - Initial delay period: Up to **30 days** following the date when the disclosure was otherwise required.
   - Additional delay period: Up to an additional **30 days** if the Attorney General determines that disclosure continues to pose a substantial risk to national security or public safety, and up to a final **60 days** in extraordinary circumstances for national security.
   - *State or local law enforcement requests or CISA notifications alone do NOT pause the 4-business-day SEC Form 8-K Item 1.05 deadline.*

3. **Exchange Pre-Notification Cross-Reference (`Nasdaq IM-5250-1`)**:
   For Nasdaq-listed issuers, filing or publicly announcing a material cybersecurity incident under `Form 8-K Item 1.05` during MarketWatch hours (`7:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. ET`) requires **10 minutes advance notification to Nasdaq MarketWatch** pursuant to `Nasdaq Rule 5250(b)(1)` and `IM-5250-1`.

4. **Amended Form 8-K for Previously Undetermined Information (`Instruction 2 to Item 1.05`)**:
   To the extent that information required by Item 1.05(a) is not determined or is unavailable at the time of the required filing, the registrant shall include a statement to this effect in the filing and then must file an **amendment to its Form 8-K (`Form 8-K/A`)** under Item 1.05 within **four (4) business days** after the registrant, without unreasonable delay, determines such information or within four business days after such information becomes available.

## Regulation S-K Item 106 & Form 10-K Item 1C — Periodic Cybersecurity Risk Management, Strategy, and Governance (`17 CFR § 229.106`)

### § 229.106(b) Risk Management and Strategy
In its Annual Report on **Form 10-K (Item 1C)**, a registrant must describe its processes, if any, for assessing, identifying, and managing material risks from cybersecurity threats in sufficient detail for a reasonable investor to understand those processes, including:
1. Whether and how the described cybersecurity processes have been integrated into the registrant's overall risk management system or processes;
2. Whether the registrant engages assessors, consultants, auditors, or other third parties in connection with any such processes; and
3. Whether the registrant has processes to oversee and identify material risks from cybersecurity threats associated with its use of any **third-party service provider** (e.g., cloud providers, market data vendors, or order management system (OMS) SaaS providers).

### § 229.106(c) Governance of Cybersecurity Risks
Registrants must describe in **Form 10-K Item 1C**:
1. **Board of Directors Oversight (`§ 229.106(c)(1)`)**: Describe the board of directors' oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats, identify any board committee or subcommittee (such as the Audit Committee under `Nasdaq Rule 5605(c)`) responsible for the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats, and describe the processes by which the board or such committee is informed about such risks.
2. **Management's Role (`§ 229.106(c)(2)`)**: Describe management's role in assessing and managing the registrant's material risks from cybersecurity threats, including which management positions or committees (e.g., Chief Information Security Officer (CISO), Chief Risk Officer) are responsible and their relevant expertise.

## Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX) Sections 302 & 404 — Disclosure Controls and Internal Control Over Financial Reporting (ICFR)

### Exchange Act Rule 13a-15 & SOX Section 302 (`17 CFR § 240.13a-14(a)`)
Every issuer registered under Section 12 of the Exchange Act must maintain:
- **Disclosure Controls and Procedures (`17 CFR § 240.13a-15(e)`)**: Quarterly evaluation by the Principal Executive Officer (CEO) and Principal Financial Officer (CFO) of the effectiveness of disclosure controls as of the end of each fiscal quarter (reported in `Form 10-Q Item 4` and `Form 10-K Item 9A`).

### SOX Section 404 & Item 308 of Regulation S-K (`17 CFR § 229.308`)
1. **Management's Annual ICFR Report (`SOX Section 404(a)`)**:
   Every Form 10-K must include an internal control report of management stating management's responsibility for establishing and maintaining adequate **Internal Control Over Financial Reporting (ICFR)** and management's assessment of the effectiveness of ICFR as of the end of the most recent fiscal year (commonly utilizing the **COSO Internal Control Framework**).
2. **Material Weakness Disclosure (`17 CFR § 229.308(a)(3)`)**:
   Management is **not permitted** to conclude that the registrant's ICFR is effective if there are **one or more material weaknesses** in the registrant's internal control over financial reporting. Any material weakness must be explicitly disclosed to investors in `Form 10-K Item 9A` and reported to the Audit Committee (`Nasdaq Rule 5605(c)`).
3. **Auditor Attestation (`SOX Section 404(b)`)**:
   For **Accelerated Filers** and **Large Accelerated Filers**, the registered public accounting firm that prepared or issued the audit report must issue an attestation report on management's assessment of ICFR. Non-accelerated filers and Emerging Growth Companies (EGCs) are exempt from `SOX 404(b)` auditor attestation but remain subject to `SOX 404(a)` management assessment.
